Shattering the Backwards Compatibility Myth: How PS5 Jailbreakers Unlocked Physical PS2, PS3, and USB Drives

Published2026.10.08
AuthorMax (McMmax) (McMmax)
Reading Time8 min read
PlatformGitHub Pages SSG
#reverse-engineering #systems #architecture

For over a decade, console manufacturers have recited the same corporate scripture: physical backwards compatibility is too technically complex, legacy architectures are too divergent to simulate, and modern consumers don't care about their vintage disc libraries.

Former PlayStation CEO Jim Ryan notoriously crystallized this sentiment in 2017 when asked about legacy game preservation: "When we've dabbled with backwards compatibility, I can say it is one of those features that is much requested, but not actually used much... they looked ancient, like why would anybody play this?"

For the PlayStation 5, Sony's official stance was firm: while PS4 discs were supported, physical PS1, PS2, and PS3 discs were deemed "unsupported." If you wanted to play legacy titles, Sony's answer was to buy their expensive PlayStation Plus Premium subscription to stream compressed PS3 games over the cloud, or re-purchase digital "Classics" remasters. On the PS5 Slim, they went a step further, requiring mandatory online DRM server pairing just to activate the detachable optical disc drive.

Over the past few weeks, the PlayStation 5 jailbreak community completely obliterated Sony's narrative.

Armed with the new Relapse kernel exploit chain, native Vulkan graphics drivers, and custom storage layer shims, homebrew developers are running authentic physical PS2 and PS3 discs directly from the console's disc tray—and even unlocking generic $20 USB optical drives on digital consoles.

Here is an engineering teardown of how jailbreakers shattered Sony's backwards compatibility myth and what it reveals about the reality of modern console hardware.

1. The Exploit Chain: From WebKit to Hypervisor Defeat

The Relapse Breakthrough

For years, public PS5 jailbreaks were stranded on early firmwares ($3.xx$ to $4.51$, stretching to $7.61$ via UMTX2). In late September 2026, the public release of the Relapse exploit chain radically shifted the landscape. By chaining a userland memory corruption vulnerability with a critical FreeBSD kernel race condition, Relapse unlocked full kernel read/write on consoles running up to firmware 13.60—including the newly released PS5 Pro.

When paired with hypervisor patchers like byepervisor, kstuff, and etaHEN, developers gained the ability to disable software sandboxes, allocate executable memory pages for emulator recompilers, and communicate directly with the console's physical optical hardware.

2. Physical PS2 Discs at 4K Resolution: PS5SX2 & 5S2CD

The moment kernel execution was stabilized, developer Sword dropped PS5SX2 (a native PS5 port of the legendary open-source PCSX2 emulator), quickly followed by The7mirrors' 5S2CD.

Their goal: insert an original physical PlayStation 2 DVD from 2004 into a PlayStation 5, press X, and play.

The Physical Truth About the Optical Drive

Sony's claim that the PS5 could not read PS2 discs was an outright fabrication. The internal optical drive inside every disc-edition PS5 is a standard Ultra-HD Blu-ray unit. By international optical drive standards, every consumer Blu-ray drive includes a secondary 650nm red laser diode specifically manufactured to read DVD-ROM media.

When a user inserts a PS2 DVD, the drive laser reads the physical disc perfectly. The reason stock PS5s refuse to play it is purely software: Sony's OS shell checks the disc's Table of Contents (TOC), sees a legacy media header, and displays an artificial warning: "Unsupported Disc."

How Modders Bypassed the Gate

The 5S2CD payload runs a lightweight background daemon (discwatch.elf). When a disc is inserted, it intercepts the optical block device, sends raw SCSI/ATAPI packet commands to stream the disc sectors directly, reads the system configuration (SYSTEM.CNF), and pulls the game's serial identifier (e.g., SLUS-20062).

The emulator then boots the game natively on the PS5's AMD Zen 2 CPU while routing the graphics through ps5vk—a native Vulkan graphics driver written for the PS5's AMD RDNA 2 GPU.

The result? Original physical PS2 games boot straight from the disc, rendering at up to 4K resolution (6x native) at a locked 60 FPS, with full RetroAchievements integration, widescreen patches, and zero input latency.

3. The CD-ROM Limitation & Generic USB Optical Drives

While PS2 DVDs (silver-bottom discs) work directly in the internal drive, early PS2 games and PS1 titles were pressed on CD-ROMs (the iconic blue-bottom discs).

Here, Sony did introduce a physical hardware limitation: to save a few cents per console in manufacturing costs, modern Blu-ray drives (in both the PS4 and PS5) omit the third 780nm infrared laser diode required to focus on legacy CD-ROM pits. The internal drive physically cannot read compact discs.

The Homebrew Answer: Generic USB Disc Readers

Instead of conceding defeat, jailbreak developers implemented custom USB Mass Storage and SCSI/ATAPI drivers directly into the homebrew layer:

  • Users can plug any generic, off-the-shelf $20 external USB DVD/Blu-ray drive into the PS5's USB ports.
  • Because external optical drives retain full CD/DVD/BD multi-laser assemblies, they read both blue-bottom PS2 CDs and silver DVDs without issue.
  • 5S2CD automatically mounts the USB optical drive as a valid storage node, dynamically rendering the inserted disc's cover art directly onto the PS5 home screen.

Bypassing the PS5 Slim Online DRM Pairing

When Sony released the PS5 Slim with its modular detachable disc drive, they introduced an anti-consumer DRM restriction: the detachable drive requires an active internet connection to authenticate with Sony's servers before it can read a single disc. If Sony's activation servers ever shut down or you are offline, your optical drive is rendered an expensive brick.

The USB drive implementation bypasses this entirely. Even PS5 Digital Edition owners can now connect a standard USB optical drive and play authentic physical media, completely bypassing Sony's server activation handshake.

4. Shattering the PS3 Cell Myth: RPCS3 Running on PS5

The most persistent corporate myth maintained by Sony was that the PlayStation 3's architecture was simply too exotic to emulate on modern consoles without dedicated hardware silicon.

Sony pointed to the PS3's notorious Cell Broadband Engine—with its single PowerPC Processing Element (PPE) and eight Synergistic Processing Elements (SPEs) sharing a high-speed Ring Bus (EIB)—claiming that emulating it on an x86_64 architecture was an insurmountable computational bottleneck. Because of this, Sony forced PS5 users into cloud-streaming PS3 games over PlayStation Plus.

The jailbreak community proved that claim completely false.

The Reality of RPCS3 on PS5

Using hypervisor-level exploits to boot customized Linux payloads (ps5-linux) and native homebrew ports (KB-RPCS3), developers ran the open-source RPCS3 emulator directly on the PS5's hardware:

  1. Thread Mapping: The PS5’s 8-core / 16-thread AMD Zen 2 processor easily allocates dedicated hardware threads to simulate the Cell’s 8 SPEs, while modern AVX2 vector instructions handle the Cell’s SIMD math with room to spare.
  2. Native Blu-ray Reading: Because PlayStation 3 games were pressed onto standard 25GB and 50GB Blu-ray media, the PS5's internal UHD drive reads physical PS3 discs natively.
  3. Performance: Demanding PS3 exclusives like MotorStorm: Pacific Rift, God of War III, and Gran Turismo 5 execute at full speed, delivering locked 60 FPS gameplay at higher resolutions than the original PS3 hardware ever achieved.

The technology was always there. Sony simply refused to build the software emulator because monetizing streaming subscriptions and digital rebundles is vastly more profitable than letting players use the physical discs they bought in 2008.

Conclusion: The Preservation Imperative

What the PlayStation 5 jailbreak scene has achieved over the past month is not just a triumph of reverse engineering—it is a masterclass in digital consumer rights.

Modern consoles are not alien computing boxes governed by mysterious physical laws. They are locked-down x86_64 PCs with custom firmware designed to enforce corporate toll booths.

When companies claim backwards compatibility is "impossible," they are talking about their quarterly balance sheets, not computational theory.

By hooking raw optical controllers, implementing native Vulkan translation drivers, and bringing open-source emulators directly to bare-metal hardware, jailbreak developers have demonstrated that true game preservation will never come from multi-billion-dollar platform holders.

It will always come from independent engineers, reverse-engineering enthusiasts, and passionate modders who believe that when you buy a game on physical media, you own it forever.
